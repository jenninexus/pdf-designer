**Trap:** a studio press-kit image titled "<Game> World Building Contest" showed two public player
posts. The first write-up called both "contest entries" and the caption said "players built and shared
their own creations" — but only ONE post said it was an entry; the other was a player *testing the
builder*. The owner caught it ("I'm skeptical about that many entries").

**Why:** the image's title framed everything under it, and the posts were summarised by theme instead
of read line by line. A composite exhibit invites plural claims it does not support.

**Rule:**
1. Read every item in the exhibit and record **what each one literally says** (date, author role, the
   exact phrase that proves the point). Keep the source path + the file's own date.
2. Write the claim at the **weakest level all items support**: "the contest was real and ran in <year>"
   — never a count, "many", or "players (plural) did X" unless the evidence counts it.
3. Add a `usage` note beside the evidence in the vault: OK phrasings and NOT-OK phrasings (counts,
   release years, player numbers), so the next document cannot drift upward.
4. Don't embed an exhibit that shows third-party handles or faces; cite it in the vault instead.

**How to apply:** any vault write sourced from media rather than an owner statement — press kits,
screenshots, store pages, social posts. Pairs with [[lesson-ask-before-calling-it-a-gap]] (don't
under-claim) — this is its mirror (don't over-claim).
