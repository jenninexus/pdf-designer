**Before building any document, test the applicant against the requirement the listing repeats.**
Whatever a listing says three or four different ways *is* the role; everything else is trim. If the
chosen applicant cannot back that spine honestly, changing applicant beats polishing prose.

**Why:** on the Sony/teamLFG **3D Character Art Lead** build (2026-07-25) the listing asked for the
same thing four ways — *"proven experience in early **AAA game development leadership**"*,
*"experience **leading art teams**"*, *"scaled content **with outsourcing companies**"*, and the
nice-to-have *"**leading multiple teams** on a live game."*

The build started for **Jenni**. Her craft match was genuinely strong (CC4 apparel, rigging, Unreal,
Maya/Blender/Max, Substance) — but her vault backs **none** of that spine at AAA scale: no AAA
team-lead, no outsourcing-vendor management, no style-guide authorship. Three real gaps against the
*core* of the role. Polishing that résumé would have produced a well-designed document making
leadership claims that collapse at the first interview question.

Switching to **Shade** answered the same bar honestly — a shipped **AAA PS5 credit**, art direction
as a `lead` claim, art-director/lead-engineer roles, 25 years of shipped product, Unreal at `lead`
strength — leaving **one** honest gap instead of three. Same listing, same day, same palette;
different person, and the application became credible.

**How to apply:**

- **Read the listing for repetition first.** Count how many ways it asks for the same capability.
  That is the spine and it decides the applicant.
- **Do the applicant test before Step 5 (theme) — not after the résumé exists.** Everything
  downstream (palette, HTML, exports, QA) is wasted if the wrong person is applying.
- **Ask the user; do not silently switch.** The applicant choice is theirs. Present the evidence for
  each candidate side by side and recommend one.
- **Never simulate seniority the vault does not back.** A credit you cannot speak to in the first
  person is a liability. Name the honest scale ("art director on shipped projects, founder running a
  studio 25 years") instead of inflating it.
- **Whose credit is it?** A studio credit is not automatically this applicant's credit. Partner-executed
  contracts (Hasbro, Halfbrick, Oddworld) are forbidden on Jenni's personal application — even framed
  as "our studio." See `docs/VAULT.md` § submission-identity scoping.
- Applying anyway with one honest gap is fine and often right — *name it once, plainly*, and pivot to
  the honest equivalent.
